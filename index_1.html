The farm

Our greenhouses sit on the highland plateau north of Nairobi, where cool nights slow the plant down and long, even days give the bloom its colour. Water comes from our own dam, fed by rain, and is recycled through the beds.

We grade every stem by hand for head size, stem length and opening stage, then pre-cool it to 2°C within an hour of cutting.

The flower

Every stem is cut by hand in the early morning at a set opening stage, so it travels tight and opens with the customer. Cool highland nights slow the plant down; long equatorial days give the bloom its depth of colour.

We grade by eye for head size, stem length and quality, then hydrate and sleeve each bunch before it goes into the cold room.

The people

Our growers, graders and packers come from the villages around the farm, and many have been with us for more than ten years. They know each variety by eye: when to cut it, how it opens, and how it should be handled.

Every bunch is graded, hydrated and sleeved by hand before it goes into the cold room, and the same team is on the pack-house floor when the trucks arrive.

From bloom to bouquet

  1. 0h

    Cut and graded

    Harvested early morning, graded by hand, into the pre-cooler at 2 °C within the hour.

  2. 0h

    Packed and sleeved

    Hydrated, sleeved in our own biodegradable sleeves, boxed and palletised in the cold room.

  3. 0h

    On the road

    About four hours in refrigerated trucks from Timau to the cargo terminal at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport.

  4. 0h

    In the air

    Into the airline's cold store, then roughly eight and a half hours non-stop to Amsterdam, or five to Dubai.

  5. 0h

    At the buyer

    On the European market within 24 to 48 hours of cutting, and on to your warehouse.
